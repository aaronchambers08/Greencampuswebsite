Causes and Effects of Climate Change

Learn how human activity is changing the planet and why action is needed now.

Climate change is mainly caused by the increase of greenhouse gases in the atmosphere. Human activities such as burning coal, oil, and gas for electricity, transport, and industry release large amounts of carbon dioxide. Deforestation also makes the problem worse because trees that would normally absorb carbon dioxide are cut down, leaving more of it in the air.

Other causes include farming practices, waste, and industrial production. Methane from livestock and landfill sites, as well as pollution from factories, all contribute to rising global temperatures. These activities trap more heat in the atmosphere and disrupt the Earth’s natural climate balance.

What Are the Effects of Climate Change?

The effects of climate change can already be seen around the world. Temperatures are rising, glaciers are melting, and sea levels are increasing. Many areas are experiencing more frequent and intense heatwaves, droughts, floods, and storms, which can damage homes, farmland, and wildlife habitats.

Climate change also affects people’s health, food supplies, and water resources. Hotter conditions can lead to illness, crop failures can make food more expensive, and changing rainfall patterns can cause water shortages. If action is not taken, these effects are likely to become even more serious for future generations.
